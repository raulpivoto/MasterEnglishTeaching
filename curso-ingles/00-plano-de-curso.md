## 1. NÍVEL BÁSICO

### 📘 Book 1 — A1 · Iniciante

**Objetivos de aprendizagem — o aluno será capaz de:**
- Cumprimentar, apresentar-se e apresentar outras pessoas.
- Compreender e usar expressões cotidianas muito familiares.
- Fazer e responder perguntas simples sobre dados pessoais, família, rotina e lugares.
- Pedir comida/bebida e comprar itens simples, com apoio de gestos e fala pausada.
- Ler placas, cardápios e mensagens curtas; preencher formulários básicos.
- Escrever frases isoladas e um cartão-postal/e-mail de 30–40 palavras.

**Principais estruturas gramaticais:**
- Verbo *to be* (afirmativo, negativo, interrogativo, respostas curtas)
- Pronomes pessoais e possessivos (*my, your, his, her…*)
- Artigos *a/an/the*; plural regular e irregulares comuns
- *This/that/these/those*; *there is/there are*
- Presente simples (rotina); advérbios de frequência
- Imperativo; *can/can't* (habilidade e permissão)
- *Wh-questions* (*what, where, who, when, how*)
- Preposições de lugar e tempo básicas (*in, on, at, next to*)
- *Have got / have*; adjetivos básicos e ordem simples
- Presente contínuo (introdução)

**Vocabulário e campos semânticos:** alfabeto e *spelling*, números 0–1.000, dias/meses/horas, países e nacionalidades, família, profissões, casa e cômodos, objetos da sala de aula, comida e bebida, roupas e cores, rotina diária, cidade e lugares, clima, hobbies.

**Funções comunicativas:** *greeting & introducing*, soletrar nome, dar telefone/e-mail, perguntar horas, descrever a casa, pedir em restaurante/café, comprar roupas, perguntar direções simples, falar de rotina, convidar e aceitar/recusar.

**Índice temático (12 unidades):**

| # | Unidade | Gramática-chave | Função principal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hello! Nice to meet you** | *to be* (I/you), alfabeto | Cumprimentar, apresentar-se |
| 2 | **Where are you from?** | *to be* (todos), nacionalidades | Falar de origem e países |
| 3 | **My family and friends** | possessivos, *'s* | Apresentar família |
| 4 | **What do you do?** | *to be* em perguntas, artigos | Falar de profissões |
| 5 | **This is my home** | *there is/are*, preposições de lugar | Descrever casa |
| 6 | **Things around me** | *this/that/these/those*, plural | Identificar objetos |
| 7 | **A day in my life** | Presente simples (I/you/we/they) | Rotina e horas |
| 8 | **He/She works…** | Presente simples (he/she/it) | Rotina de outras pessoas |
| 9 | **Food and drink** | *a/an/some*, countable/uncountable | Pedir em restaurante |
| 10 | **Let's go shopping** | *how much*, números, roupas | Comprar e perguntar preços |
| 11 | **Around town** | imperativo, *can/can't* | Pedir e dar direções simples |
| 12 | **What are you doing?** | Presente contínuo, clima | Falar do momento presente |

**Final Project:** *"My Profile"* — vídeo/áudio de 1 min + texto de 40 palavras se apresentando.

### 📗 Book 2 — A2 · Básico

**Objetivos de aprendizagem — o aluno será capaz de:**
- Comunicar-se em tarefas simples e rotineiras que exijam troca direta de informação.
- Descrever experiências passadas, planos próximos e preferências.
- Lidar com situações de viagem: hotel, transporte, restaurante, compras.
- Entender o essencial de anúncios, e-mails e notícias curtas.
- Escrever mensagens e e-mails curtos e informais (60–80 palavras).

**Principais estruturas gramaticais:**
- Passado simples (regulares, irregulares; negativas e perguntas)
- *Was/were*
- Futuro com *going to* e *will* (decisões espontâneas)
- Presente contínuo para futuro (planos)
- Comparativos e superlativos
- Contáveis/incontáveis: *much, many, a lot of, a few, a little*
- Modais: *should, must, have to, could, would like*
- Presente perfeito (introdução: experiência, *ever/never, just, already, yet*)
- Pronomes objeto; *some/any/no/every-*
- Advérbios de modo; conectores (*and, but, because, so, then*)
- Infinitivo de finalidade; *verb + -ing* (*like, love, enjoy*)

**Vocabulário e campos semânticos:** viagens e transporte, hotel e aeroporto, saúde e corpo, compras e dinheiro, esportes e lazer, tecnologia do dia a dia, trabalho e estudos, festas e tradições, meio ambiente básico, adjetivos de personalidade e aparência.

**Funções comunicativas:** fazer check-in, reservar quarto/mesa, comprar passagem, descrever sintomas e ir à farmácia, pedir/dar conselhos, fazer planos e convites, comparar produtos, contar o fim de semana, descrever uma pessoa, reclamar educadamente.

**Índice temático (12 unidades):**

| # | Unidade | Gramática-chave | Função principal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Life then and now** | *was/were*, passado simples | Falar do passado |
| 2 | **Last weekend** | passado simples (irregulares) | Contar eventos |
| 3 | **Holidays and travel** | passado, perguntas com *did* | Contar uma viagem |
| 4 | **At the airport & hotel** | *would like*, *can/could* | Check-in, reservas |
| 5 | **Eating out** | *much/many/a lot of* | Restaurante, receitas |
| 6 | **Shopping around** | comparativos | Comparar e escolher produtos |
| 7 | **The best and the worst** | superlativos | Opinar, rankings |
| 8 | **Health and body** | *should/must/have to* | Consulta, conselhos |
| 9 | **Future plans** | *going to*, presente contínuo futuro | Fazer planos e convites |
| 10 | **Predictions & decisions** | *will/won't* | Prever, prometer, decidir |
| 11 | **Have you ever…?** | presente perfeito (intro) | Falar de experiências |
| 12 | **Work, study & dreams** | revisão geral, *want to / hope to* | Entrevista simples, objetivos |

**Final Project:** *"Plan My Trip"* — roteiro escrito de 80 palavras + diálogo de reservas encenado em duplas.

## 2. NÍVEL INDEPENDENTE
*(a partir daqui, 100% das explicações em inglês)*

### 📙 Book 3 — B1 · Intermediário

**Learning objectives — the student will be able to:**
- Handle most situations while traveling or living in an English-speaking environment.
- Produce simple connected text on familiar topics (120–150 words).
- Describe experiences, events, dreams, and ambitions; give brief reasons and explanations for opinions and plans.
- Follow the main points of clear standard speech, TV news, and podcasts on familiar topics.
- Participate in conversations without preparation on everyday and personal-interest subjects.

**Key grammar structures:**
- Present perfect simple vs. past simple; present perfect continuous
- Past continuous and past simple (*when/while*); past perfect (intro)
- Future forms: *will, going to,* present continuous; future time clauses
- First and second conditionals
- Modals of obligation, advice, deduction (*must/might/can't be*)
- Passive voice (present and past simple)
- Reported speech (statements and questions)
- Relative clauses (*who, which, that, where*)
- Gerunds and infinitives; *used to / would*
- Quantifiers, articles review; phrasal verbs (high-frequency)
- Linking words (*although, however, in spite of, as a result*)

**Vocabulary & semantic fields:** work and careers, education, media and news, social media, relationships and feelings, health and lifestyle, environment, technology, travel problems, culture and traditions, money and consumption, collocations (*make/do/take/have*).

**Communicative functions:** describing experiences, narrating a story, giving opinions politely, agreeing/disagreeing, making complaints, writing formal and informal emails, handling job interviews (basic), giving short presentations, summarizing a text, asking for clarification.

**Thematic index (12 units):**

| # | Unit | Grammar focus | Main function |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Who are you?** | Tenses review, question forms | Describing yourself and others |
| 2 | **Life experiences** | Present perfect vs. past simple | Talking about experiences |
| 3 | **Tell me a story** | Past continuous, past perfect (intro) | Narrating events |
| 4 | **Work & careers** | Present perfect continuous, *for/since* | Job interviews, CVs |
| 5 | **Rules & responsibilities** | Modals of obligation & permission | Workplace/school rules |
| 6 | **What might happen?** | 1st & 2nd conditionals, *might* | Speculating, advising |
| 7 | **Made in…** | Passive voice | Describing processes |
| 8 | **News & media** | Reported speech | Reporting what others said |
| 9 | **People & places** | Relative clauses | Defining and describing |
| 10 | **Habits & memories** | *used to, would,* gerunds/infinitives | Past vs. present habits |
| 11 | **Our planet** | Future time clauses, quantifiers | Discussing environment |
| 12 | **Out in the world** | Phrasal verbs, linkers | Travel problems, complaints, emails |

**Final Project:** *"Expat Diary"* — 150-word blog post + a 2-minute spoken presentation.

### 📕 Book 4 — B2 · Independent User

**Learning objectives — the student will be able to:**
- Interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity that makes regular interaction with native speakers possible.
- Understand the main ideas of complex text on concrete and abstract topics, including technical discussions in their field.
- Produce clear, detailed text (180–250 words) on a wide range of subjects; explain a viewpoint giving advantages and disadvantages.
- Take an active part in discussions, account for and sustain opinions.
- Deliver prepared presentations and take part in meetings.

**Key grammar structures:**
- Narrative tenses mastery (past simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous)
- Future perfect and future continuous
- Second/third/mixed conditionals; *wish / if only*
- Passive (all tenses), causative (*have/get something done*)
- Reported speech and reporting verbs
- Modal verbs of speculation in past (*must have, might have, can't have*)
- Relative clauses (defining/non-defining), participle clauses
- Gerund/infinitive contrasts; *so/such, too/enough*
- Inversion (intro: *Never have I…*), emphasis (cleft sentences intro)
- Discourse markers and cohesive devices

**Vocabulary & semantic fields:** business and finance, workplace & management, science and innovation, AI and digital life, health systems, crime and justice, globalization, art and entertainment, psychology and behavior, idioms, collocations, word formation, register (formal/informal).

**Communicative functions:** negotiating, presenting data, taking part in meetings, writing reports/proposals/essays/reviews, debating, hedging and softening, expressing regret and criticism, persuading, summarizing and paraphrasing.

**Thematic index (12 units):**

| 4 | **Innovation & tech** | Passive (all forms), causative | Presenting products/processes |
| 5 | **If I had known…** | 3rd & mixed conditionals, *wish* | Expressing regret & criticism |
| 6 | **Mind & behavior** | Speculation in the past | Deducing, interpreting |
| 7 | **Money matters** | Reporting verbs, hedging | Reporting data, negotiating |
| 8 | **Law, order & ethics** | Participle clauses, relative clauses | Discussing issues, cause & effect |
| 9 | **Global village** | Gerund/infinitive contrasts | Cultural comparison, essays |
| 10 | **Arts & entertainment** | Emphasis & cleft sentences (intro) | Reviews, recommendations |
| 11 | **Health & society** | Inversion (intro), advanced linking | Argument essays |
| 12 | **The big debate** | Review; formal register | Debating & structured writing |

**Final Project:** *"Policy Brief / Panel Debate"* — 250-word argumentative essay + panel discussion.

## 3. NÍVEL PROFICIENTE

### 📓 Book 5 — C1 · Effective Operational Proficiency

**Learning objectives — the student will be able to:**
- Understand a wide range of demanding, longer texts and recognize implicit meaning.
- Express ideas fluently and spontaneously without much obvious searching for expressions.
- Use language flexibly and effectively for social, academic, and professional purposes.
- Produce clear, well-structured, detailed text (250–350 words) on complex subjects, showing controlled use of organizational patterns and connectors.
- Follow extended speech even when it is not clearly structured (films, lectures, debates).

**Key grammar structures:**
- Inversion for emphasis (*Not only… but also, Rarely do…, Had I known…*)
- Cleft sentences (*What I need is…, It was… that…*)
- Advanced conditionals (inverted, implied, *unless/provided that/supposing*)
- Subjunctive & formulaic structures (*It's essential that he be…*)
- Advanced passive & impersonal reporting (*It is said that… / He is believed to…*)
- Nominalization and complex noun phrases
- Ellipsis and substitution; fronting
- Advanced modality (*needn't have, was to have…, ought to*)
- Mixed participle & reduced clauses
- Collocation, idiomatic language, phrasal verbs (advanced)

**Vocabulary & semantic fields:** academic and professional registers, politics and diplomacy, economics, law, sciences and research, media discourse, philosophy and ethics, literature, humor & irony, advanced idioms and phrasal verbs, connotation and nuance, English varieties (UK/US/AU/etc.).

**Communicative functions:** negotiating contracts, chairing meetings, writing reports and academic essays, giving formal presentations, persuading and diplomacy, interpreting implicit attitudes, handling interviews, mediating between speakers, delivering and receiving criticism tactfully.

**Thematic index (11 units):**

| # | Unit | Grammar/Language focus | Main function |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Language & identity** | Review & diagnostic; register | Self-assessment, variety awareness |
| 2 | **Power of persuasion** | Inversion for emphasis | Persuasive speaking/writing |
| 3 | **Business without borders** | Cleft sentences, fronting | Negotiating contracts |
| 4 | **Science & the unknown** | Advanced passives, impersonal reporting | Presenting research |
| 5 | **Leadership & teamwork** | Advanced modality | Chairing/leading meetings |
| 6 | **Truth in the media** | Nominalization | Analyzing bias, writing opinion pieces |
| 7 | **Law, rights & justice** | Subjunctive, formal structures | Formal letters, legal-style texts |
| 8 | **Economy & society** | Advanced conditionals | Reports with data & forecasting |
| 9 | **Literature & the imagination** | Ellipsis, stylistic devices | Literary analysis, creative writing |
| 10 | **Humor, irony & nuance** | Idioms, connotation | Reading between the lines |
| 11 | **Academic voice** | Academic discourse markers | Academic essay & presentation |

**Final Project:** *"Position Paper"* — 350-word report + 5-minute formal presentation with Q&A.

### 📔 Book 6 — C2 · Mastery

**Learning objectives — the student will be able to:**
- Understand with ease virtually everything heard or read.
- Summarize information from different spoken and written sources, reconstructing arguments in a coherent presentation.
- Express themselves spontaneously, very fluently and precisely, differentiating finer shades of meaning even in complex situations.
- Produce sophisticated, stylistically appropriate text of any type (reviews, reports, articles, speeches).
- Mediate effectively between speakers, texts, and cultures.

**Key language focus:**
- Stylistic control: register shifting, tone, voice, rhetorical devices
- Advanced syntax: complex coordination/subordination, parallelism, balanced sentences
- Pragmatic subtlety: understatement, hedging, implicature, irony, sarcasm
- Discourse management: cohesion over long texts, signposting, argument structure
- Rare/low-frequency vocabulary: archaic, literary, technical, regional
- Idioms, proverbs, collocations, cultural allusions and wordplay
- Error-free control of complex grammar (review of residual problem areas)
- Language variation: dialects, slang, social and historical variation

**Vocabulary & semantic fields:** literature and rhetoric, philosophy and critical theory, high-level academic research, diplomacy and international relations, specialized professional jargon (law/medicine/finance/tech), pop culture & cultural references, regional and historical varieties, euphemism and political correctness.

**Communicative functions:** delivering keynote speeches, debating abstract topics at expert level, translating/mediating culturally, editing and proofreading others' writing, writing publishable-quality articles, interpreting subtext and satire, networking at professional/academic conferences, conducting complex negotiations.

**Thematic index (11 units):**

## 5. ESTRUTURA PADRÃO DE UMA UNIDADE (Template)

Toda unidade (~6–8 páginas) segue **rigorosamente** estas 6 seções, na mesma ordem e com a mesma identidade visual (ícone + cor por seção).

| # | Seção | Duração | O que contém | Adaptação A1–A2 | Adaptação B1–C2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Unit Opener** | 1 min | Título, *Can-do statements* (3–4 "Eu consigo…"), mapa da unidade | PT + EN | EN |
| 1 | 🔥 **Warm-up** | 10 min | Ativação do tema: perguntas pessoais, imagem sugerida, mini-quiz ou *brainstorm*; revisão de 2–3 itens da unidade anterior | Perguntas simples, imagens, apoio em PT | Perguntas abertas, *prediction tasks* |
| 2 | 💬 **Dialogue / Text** | 20 min | Diálogo (A1–A2) ou texto autêntico/adaptado (B1–C2) em contexto real; áudio sugerido (script incluído); 3–4 perguntas de compreensão (*gist* + detalhe) | Diálogos curtos (6–10 falas), glossário lateral | Textos variados (e-mail, artigo, entrevista, post) |
| 3 | 📚 **Vocabulary Expansion** | 20 min | 10–15 palavras-chave com definição simples, exemplo, pronúncia/IPA, tradução (só A1–A2) e imagem sugerida; 1 mini-atividade de associação; *collocations* e *word families* (B1+) | Definições em PT + exemplo EN | Definições em EN, *collocations*, registro |
| 4 | 🧩 **Grammar Point** | 25 min | Explicação direta com *Form → Use → Examples*, quadro visual, "⚠ Watch out!" (erros comuns de brasileiros), dica de pronúncia | Explicações em PT, regras curtas | Explicações em EN, nuances de uso |
| 5 | ✏️ **Practical Exercises** | 30 min | 4–6 atividades graduadas (*controlled → semi-controlled*): preencher lacunas, múltipla escolha, ligar colunas, reescrever frases, corrigir erros, ordenar palavras; **Answer Key** no fim | Foco em reconhecimento e repetição | Transformação, *error correction*, *gap-fill* aberto |
| 6 | 🎤 **Speaking / Writing Prompt** | 25 min | Desafio de produção: tarefa guiada (roteiro, frases-modelo) + checklist de autoavaliação + critérios (conteúdo, vocabulário, gramática, fluidez) | Roteiro com *sentence starters* | Tarefa aberta, rubrica detalhada |
| ✔ | **Unit Wrap-up** | 5 min | *Can-do checklist* (✓ / ~ / ✗), "Words I learned", dica de estudo extra | PT + EN | EN |

**Elementos fixos em todas as unidades:**
- 🇧🇷 **Dica Brasil** (A1–B2): armadilhas típicas de falantes de português (*false friends*, ordem de palavras).
- 🎧 **Listening/Audio:** scripts incluídos + sugestão de áudio.
- 🌎 **Culture Corner:** curiosidade cultural de 3–4 linhas.
- **Review & Checkpoint:** a cada 4 unidades (revisão + mini-teste de 20 questões + gabarito).
- **Final Project** ao término de cada apostila + **Appendices**: lista de verbos irregulares, glossário geral, tabela de fonética (IPA) e gabaritos.

**Convenções de formatação (Markdown, pronto para Word/Notion):**
- `##` título da seção; `###` subseções; **negrito** para termos-alvo; *itálico* para exemplos em inglês.
- Tabelas para vocabulário e quadros de gramática; blocos `>` para dicas e avisos.
- Lacunas: `_______`; alternativas: `a) … b) … c) …`; gabarito em seção separada.
- Ícones fixos: 🔥 💬 📚 🧩 ✏️ 🎤 ⚠ 🇧🇷 🎧 🌎 ✔

## 6. Diretrizes de Tom e Estilo

- **Tom:** encorajador, profissional, claro e acessível. Frases curtas, voz ativa, segunda pessoa ("você"/*you*).
- **Jargão:** evitar termos como "*gerund-participle*"; preferir "verbo + *-ing*". Quando indispensável, explicar com exemplo.
- **Idioma:** A1–A2 explicações em português + exemplos em inglês; B1–C2 100% em inglês, com vocabulário de instrução controlado por nível.
- **Reforço positivo:** frases como "Great job!" / "Você já consegue…" nos *Can-do*; erros tratados como parte do aprendizado.
- **Inclusão:** nomes, países e situações diversos; temas culturalmente neutros e atuais.
- **Escaneabilidade:** títulos curtos, listas, tabelas, caixas de destaque; no máximo 5 linhas de parágrafo corrido.
