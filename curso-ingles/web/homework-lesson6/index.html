Used to, be used to, get used to

What to do · about 15 min
  1. Read the three boxes slowly. Say each example out loud.
  2. Look at the comparison table. Notice what comes after to: a base verb or an -ing form?
  3. Do exercise A (choose). Check and fix.
  4. Do B (write the form) and C (error hunt).
  5. Do D: answer the conversation questions in writing, then compare with the model.
used to + base verb
a past habit or state that is not true now
✔ I used to cook for my grandmother.
✘ I didn't use to cook.
? Did you use to cook? · What did you use to cook?
be used to + -ing
to be accustomed to something (now)
✔ I am used to sleeping late.
✘ I'm not used to sleeping late.
? Are you used to sleeping late?
get used to + -ing
to become accustomed to something
✔ I need to get used to waking up earlier.
✔ She got used to living abroad.
✘ I haven't got used to the cold yet.
FormMeaningAfter "to"Example
used toI did it in the past, but not nowbase verbI used to play the guitar.
be used toIt is normal for me now-ingI'm used to playing every day.
get used toIt is becoming normal-ingI'm getting used to playing in public.
Why -ing? In be used to and get used to, the word to is a preposition, and a preposition is followed by a noun or an -ing form. In used to (past habit), to belongs to the verb, so we use the base form.
Spelling: in questions and negatives we write use to (without -d): "Did you use to live here?"

4To / Forabout 8 min

To or for?

What to do · about 8 min
  1. Read the two boxes. Say one example from each list.
  2. Do exercise A (choose to or for). Check and fix.
  3. Do B (type the word). Check and fix.

The main difference: to usually connects to a verb or to a direction or person, while for usually connects to a noun and shows benefit, purpose or duration.

TO
• destination: I'm going to Paris.
• recipient: I gave the book to her.
• + verb (purpose): I came here to help.
• time and preferences: It's quarter to two. I prefer tea to coffee.
FOR
• benefit: I bought a gift for you. Can you carry this for me?
• + noun (purpose): Let's go out for coffee. I'm studying for the exam.
• duration: I have lived here for two years.
• thanks: Thank you for your help.
